TEACHER NOTES

About this question bank

Scope and scoring

This is an independent ratio trainer for Foundation GCSE Mathematics, not a complete examination paper or an official Eduqas product. All questions are newly written. Internal demand estimates guide selection; they are not examination grades or a validated psychometric scale.

The original total-given, share-given and difference-given money questions remain. Added practice covers three-way sharing, mixtures, triangle sides, part:whole, maps, recipes, costs, an extra spending condition, and ratio reasoning. Reasoning includes simplifying ratios, common units, fractions of a whole and related quantities.

Answer generation: every numeric model answer in non-calculator practice is a whole number. Every numeric model answer in calculator practice is an exact non-integer decimal terminating after 1 or 2 decimal places. These are your chosen practice restrictions; real examinations can use other answer forms. Reasoning questions use exact choices, which can include whole-number ratios or fractions.

Numeric marking: every entered hundredth from model − 1.00 to model + 1.00 is accepted, including both boundaries. Input steps are 0.01. Entries with non-zero digits beyond two decimal places must be rounded before checking. Whole numbers, one decimal place and trailing zeros are accepted. Tolerance is applied independently to each requested amount, in its displayed unit.

This is intentionally generous. Small answers can accept zero or a negative value within the band, and near answers need not reproduce the ratio or the total exactly. The true model answer is shown after acceptance. Exact and tolerance-only results are recorded separately. Choices require the correct option: numerical tolerance cannot turn a wrong ratio or fraction into the correct statement.

One practice point is awarded when all parts of a question have been accepted. In fixed practice, a retry is permitted and accepted parts stay locked. In responsive practice, an incorrect attempt closes and the next question uses the same structure with fresh numbers. Invalid input uses no attempt. Revealed and skipped questions earn no question point. Written working is saved for review but is not automatically marked.

How questions are selected

A new responsive session starts with simplifying. A correct answer selects an explicitly linked, more demanding variation: simplifying → sharing a total → one share given → a difference given → an extra condition. There is no requirement to repeat a mastered template with bigger numbers before moving on. Further branches add different reasoning demands. Topic names, route bars, levels and selection reasons stay off the learner screen.

In responsive practice, a valid wrong submission closes that attempt. Next gives the exact same variation with fresh values; no unrelated question interrupts it. Correct parts remain in the record. Success on the fresh question advances to a harder related task, whether or not help was used; support is labelled separately. Repeated mistakes may reduce arithmetic within that same structure, never reset the whole route. Blank or invalid submissions do not change the route. At the Foundation ceiling, untested applications and coverage checks replace unlimited escalation. A short check of an earlier variation is followed by a substantial application, rather than a long tail of easy work. Advanced templates may recur with fresh numbers, separated by other tasks; successful basic templates are not repeatedly recycled. These authored rules do not predict GCSE grades.

All 36 original named variations remain available, alongside 24 additional Foundation variations (60 in total). In full practice, each must have an accepted answer to finish automatically; this is coverage, not proof of mastery. Short, timed and target sessions may finish with gaps. Teacher shows the learner on this device only and records those gaps, independent answers, retries, help and tolerance use. It is not a networked class dashboard or password-protected area.

All numeric acceptances within ±1.00 count as correct, including for selection. Near and exact acceptances are reported separately. Methods or help used before acceptance are recorded as support; opening them afterwards does not change the evidence already earned. Working space is not counted as help.

Fresh random rounds

Each new round uses a fresh random seed. Adaptive routing chooses the next variation from answer evidence; fixed routing uses a shuffled skill cycle. Numerical duplicate checks exclude the questions already in the round and up to 120 recent questions from the previous saved session. A changed name alone does not count as new mathematics. This is not a promise that no question will ever recur. All open-ended sessions have a 500-question safety limit. In an unusually long consolidation run, a small-number variation may exhaust its fresh pool: it can then reuse older mathematics while still excluding the last 24 questions and 24 recent prior-round questions, rather than unexpectedly raising difficulty or crashing.

Version 4 update · 14 October 2026: explicit correct-to-harder routes and incorrect-to-same-structure recovery. Added tasks cover combined shares, inferred ratios, percentage/remainder relationships, two price groups, coins, two-stage mixture changes and a paving-border cost. They are original questions applying Foundation skills, not a claim that all 60 forms appear on every examination.

Saving and privacy

Where permitted, progress and your optional name are stored only in this browser. This app sends no learner data and has no analytics. Shared computers may retain the previous learner’s saved session; discard it before starting. Version 4 uses a separate browser-storage key. Earlier saved rounds are left untouched; open the earlier app to review or resume them. Saving may be unavailable with local files, private browsing or restricted storage.
